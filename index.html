Assumptions and sources

  • kLa correlation. van't Riet, K. (1979). Review of measuring methods and results in nonviscous gas–liquid mass transfer in stirred vessels. Ind. Eng. Chem. Process Des. Dev. 18(3), 357–364. Water: kLa = 0.026 (Pg/V)^0.4 vs^0.5. Electrolyte solutions: kLa = 0.002 (Pg/V)^0.7 vs^0.2 (kLa in s⁻¹, Pg/V in W/m³, vs in m/s). Valid for roughly 500–10,000 W/m³, about ±40%.
  • Viscosity. van't Riet's correlation is for non-viscous liquids, so it is multiplied here by (μ/μ_water)^-n. Literature exponents for viscosity in kLa correlations range from about -0.4 to -0.7. Yagi and Yoshida (1975, Ind. Eng. Chem. Process Des. Dev. 14, 488–493) give kLa ∝ μ^-0.4. This is a Newtonian approximation. For shear-thinning broths, μ should be the apparent viscosity at the mean shear rate.
  • Temperature on kLa. kLa(T) = kLa(20 °C)·θ^(T−20), with θ = 1.024 (ASCE standard for oxygen transfer).
  • Solubility C*. Henry's law, kH(O₂) = 1.3×10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ atm⁻¹ at 25 °C with a van't Hoff term of 1700 K (Sander, 2015, Atmos. Chem. Phys. 15, 4399). Water vapour is subtracted with the Antoine equation. Salting-out uses the Sechenov relation log(C*₀/C*) = K·c with K ≈ 0.14 L/mol for NaCl, so treat it as approximate for complex media.
  • Uptake. Pirt (1965) maintenance model: qO₂ = μ/Yx/O₂ + mO. Uptake depends on dissolved oxygen through Michaelis–Menten: OUR = qO₂·X·CL/(K_O₂ + CL).
  • Power. Turbulent regime P₀ = Np·ρ·N³·D⁵ per impeller, then multiplied by Pg/P₀ for gassing. At high viscosity the flow can leave the turbulent regime and Np changes, which this tool does not model.
  • Animation. The figure is schematic. The rates and concentration levels are scaled from the model, but distances and molecule speeds are not to scale.
